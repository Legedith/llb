Supreme Court of India · 1972

Jayaram Mudaliar v Ayyaswami

(1972) 2 SCC 200

currentDirect source link
Use the holding, not the headline

This is an educational synthesis. Verify the judgment text, material facts, ratio, operative order, bench strength and later treatment before citing it.

Facts

Property subject to pending litigation was transferred and the transferee asserted rights inconsistent with the eventual decree.

Issue

How does the doctrine of lis pendens affect a transfer made during litigation?

Holding

The transfer is not void but remains subject to the rights determined in the pending proceeding; the doctrine protects the court's ability to decide effectively.

Why it matters

It is the standard authority on the operation and rationale of lis pendens.

A transferee pendente lite takes subject to the outcome of the pending litigation.

A pendente lite transferee's procedural participation does not displace the decree's binding effect.
