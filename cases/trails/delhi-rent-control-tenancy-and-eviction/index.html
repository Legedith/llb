DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Delhi rent control: tenancy, need and appellate occupation

Heritability, summary leave, bona fide need, constitutional extension and occupation charges during appeal.

1
1985 · Supreme Court of India

Gian Devi Anand v Jeevan Kumar

In the absence of a contrary statutory provision, tenancy rights and statutory protection are generally heritable; residential and commercial tenancies cannot be differentiated merely by judicial assumption.

2
1982 · Supreme Court of India

Precision Steel and Engineering Works v Prem Deva Niranjan Deva Tayal

The tenant need not prove the defence at the leave stage; an affidavit disclosing facts which, if proved, would disentitle the landlord or raise a genuine triable issue requires a trial.

3
1999 · Supreme Court of India

Shiv Sarup Gupta v Dr Mahesh Chand Gupta

The court examines the landlord's genuine, honest need from a practical perspective while testing objective facts, the nature of the premises and reasonably suitable alternative accommodation; the tenant cannot dictate the landlord's lifestyle, but the claim is not immune from scrutiny.

4
2008 · Supreme Court of India

Satyawati Sharma v Union of India

The discriminatory exclusion of commercial premises from the landlord-need remedy was read down so that the provision operated without the irrational distinction.

5
2005 · Supreme Court of India

Atma Ram Properties (P) Ltd v Federal Motors (P) Ltd

A stay can be conditioned on reasonable compensation for continued occupation after the eviction decree, reflecting the landlord's lost use rather than freezing payment at historic contractual rent.
