DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Contract interpretation, frustration and damages

How Indian contract law distinguishes construction, implied terms, supervening impossibility and compensation.

1
1954 · Supreme Court of India

Satyabrata Ghose v Mugneeram Bangur & Co

Impossibility includes impracticability that strikes at the foundation of the bargain, but the requisition on the facts did not necessarily frustrate the contract.

2
1963 · Supreme Court of India

Fateh Chand v Balkishan Das

Section 74 awards reasonable compensation up to the named amount; it does not permit automatic recovery or forfeiture disconnected from breach and legally cognisable loss.

3
2015 · Supreme Court of India

Kailash Nath Associates v Delhi Development Authority

The Court held that forfeiture requires breach and reasonable compensation; where loss is capable of proof it should be proved, and a public authority cannot retain money arbitrarily.

4
2017 · Supreme Court of India

Energy Watchdog v Central Electricity Regulatory Commission

The Court held that a mere rise in cost does not frustrate a contract; the force-majeure clause and contractual allocation of risk govern before section 56 is considered.

5
2017 · Supreme Court of India

Nabha Power Ltd v Punjab State Power Corporation Ltd

A term may be implied only where strict necessity and the recognised business-efficacy conditions are met; courts should not rewrite a negotiated commercial instrument.
