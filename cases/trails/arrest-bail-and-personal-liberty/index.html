DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Arrest, bail and personal liberty

The gradual construction of safeguards against routine arrest, custodial abuse and prolonged pre-trial detention.

1
1980 · Supreme Court of India

Gurbaksh Singh Sibbia v State of Punjab

The Court rejected inflexible formulas, treated anticipatory bail as protection of liberty through judicial discretion and required attention to the facts, seriousness, flight risk and misuse.

2
1997 · Supreme Court of India

D K Basu v State of West Bengal

The Court prescribed arrest, documentation, notification, medical examination and production safeguards, treating non-compliance as actionable and potentially contemptuous.

3
2014 · Supreme Court of India

Arnesh Kumar v State of Bihar

The Court directed police to justify arrest through statutory factors and magistrates to scrutinise detention rather than approve it mechanically.

4
2020 · Supreme Court of India

Sushila Aggarwal v State (NCT of Delhi)

Anticipatory bail need not ordinarily be time-bound and can continue until the end of trial, though courts may impose justified conditions or duration in a particular case.

5
2022 · Supreme Court of India

Satender Kumar Antil v Central Bureau of Investigation

The Court issued category-based directions, emphasised compliance with arrest safeguards, timely bail disposal and legislative consideration of a comprehensive bail law.

6
2023 · Supreme Court of India

Pankaj Bansal v Union of India

The Court required written communication of the grounds so that the arrested person can meaningfully challenge detention and seek bail.
