DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

From formal procedure to liberty, dignity and privacy

The constitutional transformation from isolated rights and formal legality to integrated, fair and proportionate rights review.

1
1950 · Supreme Court of India

A K Gopalan v State of Madras

The majority treated the rights as largely separate and read 'procedure established by law' narrowly. The compartmental approach was displaced by later decisions, especially R C Cooper and Maneka Gandhi.

2
1970 · Supreme Court of India

R C Cooper v Union of India

The Court focused on the direct effect of the measure and rejected rigid compartmentalisation of fundamental rights.

3
1978 · Supreme Court of India

Maneka Gandhi v Union of India

The Court held that procedure under Article 21 cannot be arbitrary, fanciful or oppressive; it must satisfy fairness and be tested with Articles 14 and 19 where engaged. Natural-justice requirements may be implied even when a statute is silent.

4
2017 · Supreme Court of India

Justice K S Puttaswamy (Retd) v Union of India

The Court unanimously recognised privacy as an intrinsic part of life, liberty and the freedoms in Part III, while explaining that restrictions require legality, legitimate aim and proportional safeguards.

5
2020 · Supreme Court of India

Anuradha Bhasin v Union of India

The Court held that freedom of speech and trade through the internet receive constitutional protection, indefinite suspension is impermissible, orders must be published and restrictions require periodic proportionality review.
