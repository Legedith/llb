DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Corporate personality, veil piercing, governance and insolvency

The path from separate legal personality to controlled veil piercing, minority protection and insolvency resolution.

1
1897 · House of Lords (United Kingdom)

Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd

The company was a separate legal person with its own rights and liabilities, notwithstanding concentrated ownership.

2
1986 · Supreme Court of India

Life Insurance Corporation of India v Escorts Ltd

The Court recognised that veil lifting is exceptional and depends on statute, fraud, evasion, agency or the demands of the particular legal rule; it is not a general licence to ignore incorporation.

3
1996 · Supreme Court of India

Delhi Development Authority v Skipper Construction Co

The Court disregarded the corporate structure to prevent fraud and enforce restitutionary and protective orders.

4
2019 · Supreme Court of India

Swiss Ribbons Pvt Ltd v Union of India

The Court substantially upheld the Code, treating insolvency resolution rather than debt recovery as its central objective and accepting differentiated treatment based on relevant economic roles.

5
2021 · Supreme Court of India

Tata Consultancy Services Ltd v Cyrus Investments Pvt Ltd

The Court restored the NCLT's dismissal, emphasising the statutory threshold for oppression and the limits of substituting judicial business judgment for lawful corporate governance.
