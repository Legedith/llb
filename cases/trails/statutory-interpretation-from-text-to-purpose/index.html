DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Statutory interpretation: text, context, purpose and restraint

A trail across mischief, penal strictness, whole-statute reading, purposive tax interpretation and limits on judicial supplementation.

1
1955 · Supreme Court of India

Bengal Immunity Co Ltd v State of Bihar

The prior law, defect or mischief, legislative remedy and reason for the remedy may illuminate ambiguous text, while remaining anchored to the enacted words.

2
1954 · Supreme Court of India

Tolaram Relumal v State of Bombay

A penal provision should not be extended by implication; where two reasonable constructions remain after ordinary interpretation, the one avoiding penal liability is preferred.

3
1987 · Supreme Court of India

Reserve Bank of India v Peerless General Finance and Investment Co Ltd

Interpretation depends on the text and context together; a provision should be read as part of the entire statute, its scheme and purpose rather than as an isolated sentence.

4
1981 · Supreme Court of India

K P Varghese v Income Tax Officer

The Court used text, statutory purpose and legislative history to require proof of understatement rather than taxing a fictional receipt.

5
2018 · Supreme Court of India

Commissioner of Customs (Import), Mumbai v Dilip Kumar and Company

The claimant must establish that the exemption applies; unresolved ambiguity in an exemption notification is ordinarily construed in favour of the revenue, while ambiguity in the charging provision follows different principles.

6
2013 · Supreme Court of India

Rohitash Kumar v Om Prakash Sharma

Courts cannot ordinarily supply an omission or recast legislation merely because the result seems inconvenient; correction is exceptional and must be compelled by the statute read as a whole.
