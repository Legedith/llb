DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Elections, democracy and political transparency

From electoral adjudication and Election Commission authority to candidate disclosure, disqualification, political finance and voter-roll review.

1
1975 · Supreme Court of India

Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain

The Court invalidated the immunity provision through basic-structure reasoning, emphasising free and fair elections, equality and judicial review.

2
1978 · Supreme Court of India

Mohinder Singh Gill v Chief Election Commissioner

The Court recognised broad residual election-management power within law and stated that an order must stand or fall on the reasons contained in it, not later affidavits.

3
2002 · Supreme Court of India

Union of India v Association for Democratic Reforms

The Court held that informed voting is protected by Article 19(1)(a) and directed disclosure of specified candidate information.

4
2013 · Supreme Court of India

Lily Thomas v Union of India

The Court invalidated the protection, making disqualification operate from conviction subject to lawful appellate relief.

5
2020 · Supreme Court of India

Keisham Meghachandra Singh v Hon'ble Speaker, Manipur Legislative Assembly

The Court directed time-bound adjudication and suggested reconsideration of the Speaker's institutional role, reflecting concern about strategic delay.

6
2024 · Supreme Court of India

Association for Democratic Reforms v Union of India

The Court invalidated the scheme and connected amendments, holding that the information restrictions were disproportionate and that unlimited corporate contributions created serious equality concerns.

7
2026 · Supreme Court of India

Association for Democratic Reforms v Election Commission of India

The Court upheld the revision, treated Article 324 and the Representation of the People Act as complementary, recognised a limited electoral-eligibility inquiry into citizenship and required referral of final citizenship determination to the competent authority.
