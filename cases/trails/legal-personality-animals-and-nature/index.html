DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Legal personality beyond the individual human

How Indian law uses juristic personality for deities, considers animal interests and experiments with nature personhood.

1
1969 · Supreme Court of India

Yogendra Nath Naskar v Commissioner of Income Tax

A consecrated deity may be recognised as a juristic person capable of holding property and being represented through a human manager, with legal personality serving the purposes of the endowment.

2
1999 · Supreme Court of India

Ram Jankijee Deities v State of Bihar

The juristic deity acts through a human representative, and courts distinguish the legal person, the physical idol and the manager who protects the endowed property.

3
2014 · Supreme Court of India

Animal Welfare Board of India v A Nagaraja

Animal-welfare statutes must be applied to prevent unnecessary pain and protect animal well-being; constitutional duties and an ecocentric approach may inform interpretation, although the precise constitutional status of animal rights remains doctrinally contested and later legislation must be checked.

4
2017 · Uttarakhand High Court

Mohd Salim v State of Uttarakhand

The High Court used juristic personality as a protective device, but the order's operation and practical implications were questioned and stayed in later Supreme Court proceedings. It should be studied as an ambitious but unsettled personhood experiment.

5
2019 · Supreme Court of India

M Siddiq (D) through LRs v Mahant Suresh Das

The Court treated the deity as a juristic person, assessed documentary and archaeological evidence, distinguished faith from proof of title, and fashioned relief through the law of property and constitutional authority.
