DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

International organisations: personality, powers and immunity

The development of institutional personality, implied powers, budgetary authority, staff justice and functional immunity.

1
1949 · International Court of Justice

Reparation for Injuries Suffered in the Service of the United Nations

The Court recognised objective international personality and powers necessary for the organisation to perform its functions.

2
1954 · International Court of Justice

Effect of Awards of Compensation Made by the United Nations Administrative Tribunal

The organisation's constituent framework and implied powers supported creation of an independent tribunal whose final awards bound the organisation; institutional effectiveness and justice for staff required legal effect.

3
1962 · International Court of Justice

Certain Expenses of the United Nations

The Court treated peacekeeping expenditures undertaken to fulfil UN purposes as organisational expenses, while distinguishing Security Council and General Assembly competences.

4
1999 · International Court of Justice

Difference Relating to Immunity from Legal Process of a Special Rapporteur of the Commission on Human Rights

Functional immunity attaches to words and acts performed in the course of the mission, and the competent United Nations determination carries substantial weight; the host state must respect treaty obligations and available settlement procedures.

5
1971 · International Court of Justice

Legal Consequences for States of the Continued Presence of South Africa in Namibia

South Africa's continued presence was illegal; states were obliged not to recognise or assist the unlawful situation while taking account of the interests of Namibia's people.
