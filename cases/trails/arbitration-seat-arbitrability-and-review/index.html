DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

Arbitration: seat, arbitrability, appointment and review

The modern Indian move toward seat-centred arbitration, limited court intervention and structurally impartial tribunals.

1
2012 · Supreme Court of India

BALCO v Kaiser Aluminium Technical Services Inc

The Constitution Bench adopted the territoriality principle and held prospectively that Part I does not apply to foreign-seated arbitrations, subject to later statutory amendments for limited interim support.

2
2011 · Supreme Court of India

Booz Allen and Hamilton Inc v SBI Home Finance Ltd

The Court distinguished rights in personam from rights in rem and held that mortgage enforcement through sale was not arbitrable in the circumstances.

3
2019 · Supreme Court of India

Perkins Eastman Architects DPC v HSCC (India) Ltd

The Court held that a person interested in the outcome cannot retain exclusive power to appoint the sole arbitrator.

4
2020 · Supreme Court of India

Vidya Drolia v Durga Trading Corporation

The Court formulated a fourfold non-arbitrability test and adopted a restrained prima facie referral review, while treating certain rent disputes under special statutes differently from ordinary tenancy claims.

5
2019 · Supreme Court of India

Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co Ltd v National Highways Authority of India

The Court narrowed public-policy review, explained patent illegality for domestic awards and set aside the relevant part because the tribunal effectively created a new bargain contrary to the contract and natural justice.

6
2023 · Supreme Court of India

N N Global Mercantile Pvt Ltd v Indo Unique Flame Ltd

The majority held that non-stamping is a curable defect and does not render the arbitration agreement void or non-existent; referral courts should not derail arbitration through a full stamp inquiry.

7
2021 · Supreme Court of India

PASL Wind Solutions Pvt Ltd v GE Power Conversion India Pvt Ltd

Party autonomy permits Indian parties to choose a foreign seat absent a specific statutory prohibition; an award made there can be a foreign award, while mandatory Indian law may still affect the underlying transaction and available relief.
