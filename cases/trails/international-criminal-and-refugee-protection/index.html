DEVELOPMENT TRAIL

International criminal law and refugee protection

Classification of conflict, individual criminal responsibility and the protection of people fleeing persecution.

1
1995 · International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia

Prosecutor v Dusko Tadic (Jurisdiction Decision)

The Appeals Chamber articulated a broad armed-conflict threshold and upheld the tribunal's establishment and jurisdiction.

2
1998 · International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda

Prosecutor v Jean-Paul Akayesu

The Trial Chamber entered the first international conviction for genocide and recognised rape and sexual violence as capable of constituting acts of genocide when committed with the required intent.

3
2012 · International Criminal Court

Prosecutor v Thomas Lubanga Dyilo

The ICC convicted Lubanga and later developed victim-participation and reparation principles, while debate continued about charging strategy and sexual violence evidence.

4
1996 · Supreme Court of India

National Human Rights Commission v State of Arunachal Pradesh

The Court required protection of life and liberty, restrained forcible eviction except through lawful process and directed authorities to process citizenship applications according to law.

5
1991 · Supreme Court of India

Louis De Raedt v Union of India

The Court recognised Article 21 protection but held that the citizen-specific right to reside and settle does not extend to foreigners, whose stay remains governed by law.
