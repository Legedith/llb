Supreme Court of India · 1986

Central Inland Water Transport Corporation v Brojo Nath Ganguly

(1986) 3 SCC 156

current with contextDirect source link
Use the holding, not the headline

This is an educational synthesis. Verify the judgment text, material facts, ratio, operative order, bench strength and later treatment before citing it.

Facts

A public-sector employer relied on a service rule permitting termination of permanent employees by notice without assigning cause.

Issue

Whether an unconscionable standard-form term imposed through unequal bargaining power is enforceable.

Holding

The Court refused to enforce the oppressive clause, treating it as opposed to public policy and arbitrary in the setting of a state instrumentality.

Why it matters

It is a leading Indian authority on unconscionability, unequal bargaining power and public-law control of contract.

PROPOSITION MAP

Where this decision fits in the course

Each connection names the proposition for which the case is being used. A node link does not imply that every sentence of the judgment controls that topic.

Anchor authority · LB-102Public policy

An unconscionable term imposed through gross inequality of bargaining power may be void as against public policy.

Anchor authority · LB-102Freedom of contract and its limits

Freedom of contract is not a licence to enforce oppressive standard terms.

Application · LB-402Public-law contracts and tenders

State instrumentalities remain subject to non-arbitrariness in contractual action.
