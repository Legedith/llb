Supreme Court of India · 1997

Vishaka v State of Rajasthan

[1997] Supp 3 SCR 404

current with legislationDirect source link
Use the holding, not the headline

This is an educational synthesis. Verify the judgment text, material facts, ratio, operative order, bench strength and later treatment before citing it.

Facts

Women's groups sought enforceable protection after the gang rape of Bhanwari Devi, a social worker, exposed the absence of a domestic workplace-sexual-harassment framework.

Issue

Whether constitutional courts could formulate binding safeguards against workplace sexual harassment in the absence of legislation.

Holding

The Court held that workplace sexual harassment violates equality, dignity, liberty and occupational freedom, used international norms consistent with fundamental rights, and issued binding guidelines until legislation was enacted.

Why it matters

It links constitutional rights, CEDAW, judicial remedies and the statutory framework later enacted in 2013.
