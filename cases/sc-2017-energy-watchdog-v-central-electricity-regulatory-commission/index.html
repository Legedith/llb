Supreme Court of India · 2017

Energy Watchdog v Central Electricity Regulatory Commission

(2017) 14 SCC 80

currentDirect source link
Use the holding, not the headline

This is an educational synthesis. Verify the judgment text, material facts, ratio, operative order, bench strength and later treatment before citing it.

Facts

Power generators sought relief after imported coal became more expensive because of a change in Indonesian regulation.

Issue

Whether commercial hardship and a change in input cost trigger force majeure or frustration.

Holding

The Court held that a mere rise in cost does not frustrate a contract; the force-majeure clause and contractual allocation of risk govern before section 56 is considered.

Why it matters

It is the modern commercial authority on force majeure, hardship and contractual risk allocation.
