House of Lords (United Kingdom) · 1860

Cox v Hickman

(1860) 8 HL Cas 268

persuasiveDiscovery link — verify
Use the holding, not the headline

This is an educational synthesis. Verify the judgment text, material facts, ratio, operative order, bench strength and later treatment before citing it.

Facts

Creditors of an insolvent business participated in arrangements for continuing the enterprise and were sued as alleged partners.

Issue

Does sharing profits automatically create partnership and liability as partners?

Holding

The House of Lords rejected profit sharing as a conclusive test and focused on whether the business was carried on on behalf of the alleged partners through a relationship of agency.

Why it matters

It is the classical source of the real-relation and mutual-agency tests used in Indian partnership law.
