2023 · Supreme Court of India · 2023 INSC 866

Pankaj Bansal v Union of India

The Court required written communication of the grounds so that the arrested person can meaningfully challenge detention and seek bail.

It is the key procedural qualification on PMLA arrest after Vijay Madanlal.

2022 · Supreme Court of India · 2022 INSC 1280

Neeraj Dutta v State (Government of NCT of Delhi)

The Court held that demand and acceptance remain essential but may be proved through direct or circumstantial evidence; a hostile or unavailable complainant does not automatically end the prosecution.

It is the present leading authority on proof in corruption cases.

2022 · Supreme Court of India · 2022 SCC OnLine SC 929

Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v Union of India

The Court substantially upheld the framework and adopted a broad reading of the continuing process or activity connected with proceeds of crime, while certain later cases have imposed procedural safeguards.

It is the primary structural authority on PMLA, but every proposition must be checked against later judgments and legislative changes.

Tofan Singh v State of Tamil Nadu

The majority held that such officers are police officers for the relevant evidentiary rule and that confessional statements to them are inadmissible as confessions.

It is the leading authority on NDPS confessions and the relationship between special investigation and evidence law.

2008 · Supreme Court of India · (2008) 16 SCC 417

Noor Aga v State of Punjab

The prosecution must first establish foundational facts and substantial compliance with safeguards before reverse burdens operate; the severe statute demands careful proof.

It is a major authority on NDPS burden shifting and procedural fairness.

2006 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

SEBI v Shriram Mutual Fund

Where the statute creates a civil obligation and prescribed monetary consequence, contravention may attract penalty without proof of criminal mens rea, subject to the text, scheme and any statutory discretion.

It is a principal authority for strict civil regulatory liability.

1966 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1966 SC 43

Nathulal v State of Madhya Pradesh

The Court applied the presumption of mens rea and acquitted because the accused acted under an honest belief inconsistent with the required guilty mind.

It is the standard counterpoint to Mayer Hans George.

1965 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1965 SC 722

State of Maharashtra v Mayer Hans George

The Court upheld liability in the statutory setting, treating the offence as one where the legislative scheme displaced the ordinary mens rea presumption.

It is the leading Indian example of statutory strict liability and the need to construe each offence's text and purpose.
