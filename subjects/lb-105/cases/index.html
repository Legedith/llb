2018 · Supreme Court of India · (2018) 16 SCC 368

Shafin Jahan v Asokan K M

The Supreme Court restored the marriage and affirmed that adult choice in marriage forms part of liberty, dignity and autonomy.

It is a leading authority on decisional autonomy, habeas jurisdiction and family control.

Amardeep Singh v Harveen Kaur

The Court treated the period as directory and waivable when reconciliation is impossible, separation requirements are met and delay would only prolong hardship.

2017 · Supreme Court of India · [2017] 9 SCR 797

Shayara Bano v Union of India

A majority set aside talaq-e-biddat, though the judges used different routes, including arbitrariness and the status of the practice within personal law.

It is essential for Muslim divorce law, personal-law review and the relationship between religion, equality and legislation.

The Court offered illustrative, non-exhaustive categories and stressed cumulative context, relationship history and the gravity of conduct.

Shamim Ara v State of Uttar Pradesh

A mere assertion in a written statement is insufficient; talaq must be proved as pronounced for reasonable cause and preceded by an attempt at reconciliation in the framework adopted by the Court.

Danial Latifi v Union of India

The Court upheld the Act by construing it to require a fair provision made within iddat that can meet the divorced woman's future needs beyond that period.

1999 · Supreme Court of India · [1999] 1 SCR 669

Githa Hariharan v Reserve Bank of India

The Court interpreted 'after' as meaning in the father's absence from care, not only after his death, thereby avoiding a discriminatory hierarchy.

It is a leading gender-equal and rights-consistent interpretation of guardianship.

Sarla Mudgal v Union of India

Conversion does not automatically dissolve the subsisting marriage; a second marriage during its continuance can attract bigamy consequences.

Y Narasimha Rao v Y Venkata Lakshmi

Recognition ordinarily requires jurisdiction and a ground of relief recognised by the matrimonial law governing the parties, a decision on merits, fair notice and opportunity, and absence of fraud or breach of Indian law.

Mohd Ahmed Khan v Shah Bano Begum

The Court held that the remedy applied irrespective of religion and that the husband's personal-law obligations did not exclude the statutory duty.

1975 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Satya v Teja Singh

A foreign judgment founded on a fraudulent assumption of jurisdiction or denial of a real opportunity to contest is not entitled to recognition merely because it is formally valid in the foreign forum.

It is a foundational Indian conflict-of-laws case on fraud, domicile and matrimonial recognition.

1903 · Judicial Committee of the Privy Council · [1903] UKPC 12

Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose

An agreement with a minor is void from the beginning because a minor is not competent to contract; restitution cannot be used to enforce the void bargain indirectly.

It is the foundational capacity case and must be read with statutory rules on necessaries and restitution.
