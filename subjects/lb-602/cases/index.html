2023 · Supreme Court of India · 2023 INSC 1066

N N Global Mercantile Pvt Ltd v Indo Unique Flame Ltd

The majority held that non-stamping is a curable defect and does not render the arbitration agreement void or non-existent; referral courts should not derail arbitration through a full stamp inquiry.

It is the current larger-bench authority connecting separability, referral and stamp law.

2021 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

PASL Wind Solutions Pvt Ltd v GE Power Conversion India Pvt Ltd

Party autonomy permits Indian parties to choose a foreign seat absent a specific statutory prohibition; an award made there can be a foreign award, while mandatory Indian law may still affect the underlying transaction and available relief.

It connects seat theory, party autonomy, enforcement and domestic mandatory law.

2020 · Supreme Court of India · (2021) 2 SCC 1

Vidya Drolia v Durga Trading Corporation

The Court formulated a fourfold non-arbitrability test and adopted a restrained prima facie referral review, while treating certain rent disputes under special statutes differently from ordinary tenancy claims.

It is the present leading framework for arbitrability and referral review.

Perkins Eastman Architects DPC v HSCC (India) Ltd

The Court held that a person interested in the outcome cannot retain exclusive power to appoint the sole arbitrator.

It is the leading modern authority on unilateral appointments and arbitral independence.

2019 · Supreme Court of India · [2019] 7 SCR 522

Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co Ltd v National Highways Authority of India

The Court narrowed public-policy review, explained patent illegality for domestic awards and set aside the relevant part because the tribunal effectively created a new bargain contrary to the contract and natural justice.

It is the leading post-2015 award-review authority.

2012 · Supreme Court of India · (2012) 9 SCC 552

BALCO v Kaiser Aluminium Technical Services Inc

The Constitution Bench adopted the territoriality principle and held prospectively that Part I does not apply to foreign-seated arbitrations, subject to later statutory amendments for limited interim support.

It is the foundational Indian seat doctrine.

2011 · Supreme Court of India · (2011) 5 SCC 532

Booz Allen and Hamilton Inc v SBI Home Finance Ltd

The Court distinguished rights in personam from rights in rem and held that mortgage enforcement through sale was not arbitrable in the circumstances.

It is the classic Indian arbitrability framework, later refined by Vidya Drolia.

2011 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

SMS Tea Estates Pvt Ltd v Chandmari Tea Co Pvt Ltd

The decision treated the unstamped instrument as requiring impounding before substantive reliance, while recognising separability of the arbitration clause. Its arbitration-stamping consequences were later reconsidered and materially displaced by larger-bench authority.

It is important as a treatment-history case showing how doctrine changes.

2005 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Salem Advocate Bar Association, Tamil Nadu v Union of India (II)

The Court upheld and operationalised major procedural reforms, issued implementation guidance and treated case management, realistic costs and ADR referral as tools for reducing delay while preserving fairness.

It is a bridge between CPC doctrine, case management and institutional ADR.

currentDirect source link
1994 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Renusagar Power Co Ltd v General Electric Co

For foreign awards, public policy was confined to fundamental policy, the interests of India and justice or morality, rather than a merits review. Later legislation and cases refined the language but preserved a narrow enforcement orientation.

It is the starting point for India's pro-enforcement public-policy doctrine.
