2020 · Supreme Court of India · (2020) 3 SCC 637

Anuradha Bhasin v Union of India

The Court held that freedom of speech and trade through the internet receive constitutional protection, indefinite suspension is impermissible, orders must be published and restrictions require periodic proportionality review.

It is the principal Indian authority on internet shutdowns, publication of executive orders and digital proportionality.

2020 · Supreme Court of India · (2020) 7 SCC 1

Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal

The Court reaffirmed the certificate requirement for secondary electronic evidence, clarified that it may be produced at an appropriate stage and recognised court assistance where the record is controlled by another person.

It is the present leading authority on electronic-record certification under the predecessor statute, requiring crosswalk to the BSA.

2017 · Supreme Court of India · [2017] 10 SCR 569

Justice K S Puttaswamy (Retd) v Union of India

The Court unanimously recognised privacy as an intrinsic part of life, liberty and the freedoms in Part III, while explaining that restrictions require legality, legitimate aim and proportional safeguards.

It supplies the constitutional foundation for bodily autonomy, informational privacy, dignity, sexuality and data governance.

2015 · Supreme Court of India · [2015] 5 SCR 963

Shreya Singhal v Union of India

The Court struck down section 66A for vagueness and overbreadth, upheld section 69A with safeguards and read actual knowledge for intermediary takedown as requiring court or authorised governmental process.

It is the foundational case for online speech, vagueness, blocking and intermediary safe harbour.

2014 · Supreme Court of India · (2014) 10 SCC 473

Anvar P V v P K Basheer

The Court required compliance with the electronic-record certificate provision for the relevant category of secondary electronic evidence and displaced contrary reasoning in State v Navjot Sandhu.

It reset Indian electronic-evidence doctrine and was later clarified by Arjun Panditrao.

2014 · Supreme Court of India · [2014] 5 SCR 119

National Legal Services Authority v Union of India

The Court recognised transgender persons as entitled to constitutional equality, dignity and self-identification, and directed affirmative governmental measures.

It is the foundational Indian authority on gender identity and transgender constitutional rights.

2010 · Supreme Court of India · (2010) 3 SCC 1

Trimex International FZE Ltd v Vedanta Aluminium Ltd

The Court treated the essential terms and unconditional electronic acceptance as capable of creating a binding contract; contemplation of a formal document did not necessarily postpone formation.

It is a practical authority for email contracting and the difference between agreement and later documentation.

Satyam Infoway Ltd v Sifynet Solutions Pvt Ltd

1965 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1966 SC 543

Bhagwandas Goverdhandas Kedia v Girdharilal Parshottamdas

For instantaneous communications, the contract is made where acceptance is received and heard, unlike the traditional postal rule.

It is the Indian anchor for instantaneous acceptance and territorial jurisdiction in communications contracts.
