2020 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Dahiben v Arvindbhai Kalyanji Bhanusali

The court examines the plaint as a whole and the documents relied on by the plaintiff, assuming pleaded facts for the limited inquiry; a clever drafting device cannot avoid rejection where the bar is apparent, but disputed defence material is not tried at this stage.

It clarifies the narrow, document-focused character of rejection at the threshold.

Courts may adopt a justice-oriented approach, but sufficient cause still requires a bona fide, reasonable and adequately explained account; limitation cannot be displaced by sympathy or vague assertions.

Revajeetu Builders and Developers v Narayanaswamy and Sons

Courts should consider whether the amendment is necessary for the real controversy, bona fide, compensable in costs, prejudicial beyond repair, limitation-sensitive or transformative of the case; the post-trial due-diligence requirement must also be respected.

It supplies an exam-ready factor matrix for pleading amendments.

Atma Ram Properties (P) Ltd v Federal Motors (P) Ltd

A stay can be conditioned on reasonable compensation for continued occupation after the eviction decree, reflecting the landlord's lost use rather than freezing payment at historic contractual rent.

The time prescription was treated as directory in the general CPC setting, with extension reserved for exceptional reasons and not routine indulgence.

Salem Advocate Bar Association, Tamil Nadu v Union of India (II)

The Court upheld and operationalised major procedural reforms, issued implementation guidance and treated case management, realistic costs and ADR referral as tools for reducing delay while preserving fairness.

Karnataka Board of Wakf v Government of India

Possession must be actual, open, continuous, exclusive and hostile to the true owner's title for the statutory period; the claimant must identify when and how hostility began and cannot rely on vague long possession alone.

It supplies the pleading and proof checklist for adverse possession.

2002 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Central Bank of India v Ravindra

Interest may be capitalised only according to contract, banking practice and law; penal interest cannot ordinarily be capitalised in the same way, and courts must distinguish pre-suit, pendente lite and post-decree interest.

It is the leading conceptual map for interest calculations in banking litigation.

L Chandra Kumar v Union of India

The Court held that judicial review under Articles 226, 227 and 32 is part of the basic structure; tribunals may act as courts of first instance but remain subject to High Court scrutiny.

It is the foundational authority on tribunals, ouster clauses and basic-structure judicial review.

Wander Ltd v Antox India P Ltd

Appellate interference is limited where the first court exercised discretion judicially on relevant principles; a different possible view is not enough unless the order is arbitrary, capricious, perverse or legally mistaken.

The transfer is not void but remains subject to the rights determined in the pending proceeding; the doctrine protects the court's ability to decide effectively.

1960 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Satyadhyan Ghosal v Deorajin Debi

The principle of finality can apply between stages of the same litigation so that an issue finally decided at an earlier stage is not ordinarily reopened at a later stage, subject to appellate correction and the procedural setting.

It connects the policy of finality to the internal life of a suit, not only to later suits.

1955 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Sangram Singh v Election Tribunal, Kotah

Procedure is designed to advance justice rather than create traps, but a court must still respect the text, the stage reached, the other side's accrued procedural position and the conditions for setting aside default.

The judgment is frequently used to explain why procedural fairness is not the same as ignoring procedural rules.

currentDirect source link
1954 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Kiran Singh v Chaman Paswan

A defect of subject-matter or inherent jurisdiction strikes at the court's authority and ordinarily renders the decree a nullity. Statutory rules may restrict late objections to territorial or pecuniary jurisdiction by requiring timely objection and consequent failure of justice.

The case is a foundational map for separating inherent lack of jurisdiction from waivable forum objections.
