2019 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Ltd v Govindan Raghavan

A dominant developer cannot rely on manifestly one-sided terms to defeat consumer relief; delay and unfair allocation of remedies may justify refund, interest and compensation under consumer law.

It links adhesion contracts, housing regulation and consumer remedies.

2005 · Supreme Court of India · (2005) 6 SCC 1

Jacob Mathew v State of Punjab

Criminal negligence requires a very high degree of grossness; professional conduct is assessed against an ordinarily competent practitioner, and prosecution should not be launched casually.

It is the central Indian authority distinguishing civil medical negligence from criminal negligence.

1998 · Supreme Court of India · (1998) 4 SCC 39

Spring Meadows Hospital v Harjol Ahluwalia

The hospital and responsible medical personnel were liable; both the child beneficiary and parents who hired the service were recognised within the consumer framework.

It is a practical consumer-medical-negligence case on vicarious liability and compensation.

1995 · Supreme Court of India · (1995) 6 SCC 651

Indian Medical Association v V P Shantha

Medical services for consideration generally fall within consumer jurisdiction, subject to exclusions for wholly free service and contracts of personal service.

It opened a major remedial route for patients and remains important under the 2019 Act.

Nilabati Behera v State of Orissa

The Court awarded compensation for custodial death and distinguished constitutional public-law liability from private-law tort remedies.

It is the principal bridge between custodial rights, constitutional tort and compensation.

1987 · Supreme Court of India · (1987) 1 SCC 395

M C Mehta v Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak)

The Court formulated absolute and non-delegable enterprise liability, without the traditional Rylands exceptions, and linked compensation to the enterprise's capacity and the magnitude of harm.

It is the foundational Indian authority on absolute liability, constitutional tort technique and hazardous industry.

1966 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1966 SC 1750

Municipal Corporation of Delhi v Subhagwanti

The Court applied res ipsa loquitur and held the authority liable because the occurrence itself strongly indicated lack of proper care.

It is the standard Indian illustration of res ipsa loquitur and public-authority negligence.

1965 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1965 SC 1039

Kasturi Lal Ralia Ram Jain v State of Uttar Pradesh

The majority denied damages under the then sovereign-function doctrine, while recognising negligence. Later constitutional-tort cases sharply limited the practical reach of this immunity.

It is a historical authority that must be read against Nilabati Behera and modern public-law compensation.

Donoghue v Stevenson

The House of Lords recognised a duty based on reasonable foreseeability and proximity, expressed through the neighbour principle.

It is the comparative foundation for modern negligence and manufacturer liability.

1868 · House of Lords (United Kingdom) · (1868) LR 3 HL 330

Rylands v Fletcher

The defendant may be liable for non-natural use, accumulation and escape, subject to recognised exceptions.

It is the historical strict-liability rule from which Indian absolute liability deliberately departs.
