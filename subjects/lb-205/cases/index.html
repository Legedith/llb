2003 · Supreme Court of India · (2004) 10 SCC 1

Union of India v Azadi Bachao Andolan

The Court upheld the circular and rejected the proposition that every tax-avoidance arrangement is illegitimate; legal form and treaty entitlement matter unless the law supplies a contrary rule.

It is the principal corrective to overbroad readings of McDowell.

2002 · International Court of Justice · ICJ Reports 2002, p 3

Arrest Warrant of 11 April 2000 (Democratic Republic of the Congo v Belgium)

The Court held that incumbent high-ranking officials enjoy personal immunity before foreign national courts while in office, without equating immunity with impunity.

It is central to universal jurisdiction, immunities and the distinction between jurisdictional bar and substantive responsibility.

1997 · Supreme Court of India · [1997] Supp 3 SCR 404

Vishaka v State of Rajasthan

The Court held that workplace sexual harassment violates equality, dignity, liberty and occupational freedom, used international norms consistent with fundamental rights, and issued binding guidelines until legislation was enacted.

It links constitutional rights, CEDAW, judicial remedies and the statutory framework later enacted in 2013.

1991 · Supreme Court of India · (1991) 3 SCC 554

Louis De Raedt v Union of India

The Court recognised Article 21 protection but held that the citizen-specific right to reside and settle does not extend to foreigners, whose stay remains governed by law.

It is an important boundary case for refugee and migration claims.

1986 · International Court of Justice · ICJ Reports 1986, p 14

Military and Paramilitary Activities in and against Nicaragua (Nicaragua v United States)

The Court found violations of the prohibition on force and non-intervention, distinguished treaty from customary obligations and required conditions such as an armed attack and request for collective self-defence.

It is the central modern authority on use of force, non-intervention, custom and attribution.

1971 · International Court of Justice · Citation to verify

Legal Consequences for States of the Continued Presence of South Africa in Namibia

South Africa's continued presence was illegal; states were obliged not to recognise or assist the unlawful situation while taking account of the interests of Namibia's people.

The opinion is central to Security Council authority, non-recognition and legal consequences of institutional decisions.

1970 · International Court of Justice · ICJ Reports 1970, p 3

Barcelona Traction, Light and Power Company, Limited

The Court generally linked corporate diplomatic protection to the State of incorporation and distinguished obligations owed to the international community as a whole.

It is foundational for diplomatic protection, corporate nationality and obligations erga omnes.

1969 · International Court of Justice · ICJ Reports 1969, p 3

North Sea Continental Shelf Cases

The Court required extensive and virtually uniform state practice accompanied by opinio juris and rejected automatic binding force of the treaty rule on the non-party in the circumstances.

It is the canonical case on customary international law and maritime delimitation.

1927 · Permanent Court of International Justice · PCIJ Series A No 10

S S Lotus (France v Turkey)

The Court adopted a permissive approach: restrictions on state jurisdiction cannot simply be presumed, though later law and treaties qualify the decision's broad language.

It is a classic starting point for jurisdiction, sovereignty and the evolution of international law.
