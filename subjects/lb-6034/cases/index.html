2023 · Supreme Court of India · 2023 INSC 1066

N N Global Mercantile Pvt Ltd v Indo Unique Flame Ltd

The majority held that non-stamping is a curable defect and does not render the arbitration agreement void or non-existent; referral courts should not derail arbitration through a full stamp inquiry.

It is the current larger-bench authority connecting separability, referral and stamp law.

2016 · Supreme Court of India · (2016) 10 SCC 767

Satya Pal Anand v State of Madhya Pradesh

The Court held that the registration authority's powers are limited; substantive invalidity or cancellation generally requires proceedings before the competent civil court.

It is a practical authority on registration officers, title disputes and remedies.

2011 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

SMS Tea Estates Pvt Ltd v Chandmari Tea Co Pvt Ltd

The decision treated the unstamped instrument as requiring impounding before substantive reliance, while recognising separability of the arbitration clause. Its arbitration-stamping consequences were later reconsidered and materially displaced by larger-bench authority.

It is important as a treatment-history case showing how doctrine changes.

Such arrangements do not convey title or amount to a completed transfer, though genuine powers of attorney, development arrangements and contracts may retain their lawful effects.

Balwant Singh v Jagdish Singh

Courts may adopt a justice-oriented approach, but sufficient cause still requires a bona fide, reasonable and adequately explained account; limitation cannot be displaced by sympathy or vague assertions.

K B Saha and Sons Pvt Ltd v Development Consultant Ltd

An unregistered instrument required to be registered cannot prove the transaction affecting immovable property, but may be admitted for a genuinely collateral matter independent of the transaction that required registration.

It supplies a practical test for the often-misused collateral-purpose exception.

Rupa Ashok Hurra v Ashok Hurra

To prevent a gross miscarriage of justice in exceptional circumstances, the Court recognised a tightly confined curative petition following review, subject to certification, circulation and demanding threshold conditions.

1995 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Bhoop Singh v Ram Singh Major

The statutory exemption for decrees does not permit parties to use a consent decree as a device for creating rights in property outside the subject matter of the suit without registration.

It explains the boundary between exempt decrees and registrable compromise arrangements.

1969 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Hindustan Steel Ltd v Dilip Construction Co

Stamp law secures revenue and provides a cure mechanism; penalty should be applied according to the statute and the character of the default rather than as an automatic weapon to defeat a genuine transaction.

It connects revenue protection, admissibility and proportional response to defects.

1955 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Sangram Singh v Election Tribunal, Kotah

Procedure is designed to advance justice rather than create traps, but a court must still respect the text, the stage reached, the other side's accrued procedural position and the conditions for setting aside default.

The judgment is frequently used to explain why procedural fairness is not the same as ignoring procedural rules.
