Pankaj Bansal v Union of India

The Court required written communication of the grounds so that the arrested person can meaningfully challenge detention and seek bail.

Satender Kumar Antil v Central Bureau of Investigation

The Court issued category-based directions, emphasised compliance with arrest safeguards, timely bail disposal and legislative consideration of a comprehensive bail law.

2020 · Supreme Court of India · (2020) 5 SCC 1

Sushila Aggarwal v State (NCT of Delhi)

Anticipatory bail need not ordinarily be time-bound and can continue until the end of trial, though courts may impose justified conditions or duration in a particular case.

It is the present leading treatment of anticipatory-bail duration.

1950 · Supreme Court of India · [1950] SCR 88

A K Gopalan v State of Madras

The majority treated the rights as largely separate and read 'procedure established by law' narrowly. The compartmental approach was displaced by later decisions, especially R C Cooper and Maneka Gandhi.

It is indispensable as a historical starting point for the evolution of Article 21 and for understanding how precedent can be narrowed or overruled.

1947 · Judicial Committee of the Privy Council · AIR 1947 PC 67

Pulukuri Kottaya v Emperor

Only so much of the information as distinctly relates to the fact discovered is admissible; the exception does not admit a full confession merely because discovery follows.

It is the controlling foundation for discovery statements.
