2021 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

PASL Wind Solutions Pvt Ltd v GE Power Conversion India Pvt Ltd

Party autonomy permits Indian parties to choose a foreign seat absent a specific statutory prohibition; an award made there can be a foreign award, while mandatory Indian law may still affect the underlying transaction and available relief.

It connects seat theory, party autonomy, enforcement and domestic mandatory law.

2017 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Alcon Electronics Pvt Ltd v Celem SA

A judgment from a competent foreign court may be conclusive and executable when it is on merits and does not fall within the statutory exceptions concerning natural justice, fraud, incorrect jurisdictional foundation or breach of Indian law.

The case provides a practical foreign-judgment enforcement example beyond matrimonial litigation.

2012 · Supreme Court of India · (2012) 9 SCC 552

BALCO v Kaiser Aluminium Technical Services Inc

The Constitution Bench adopted the territoriality principle and held prospectively that Part I does not apply to foreign-seated arbitrations, subject to later statutory amendments for limited interim support.

It is the foundational Indian seat doctrine.

2003 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Modi Entertainment Network v WSG Cricket Pte Ltd

An anti-suit injunction is an equitable order against a party, not the foreign court. It requires personal jurisdiction, caution, comity, prevention of injustice and a strong case that the chosen foreign proceeding is oppressive, vexatious or contrary to a binding forum arrangement.

It is the principal Indian framework for anti-suit injunctions and forum clauses.

1994 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Renusagar Power Co Ltd v General Electric Co

For foreign awards, public policy was confined to fundamental policy, the interests of India and justice or morality, rather than a merits review. Later legislation and cases refined the language but preserved a narrow enforcement orientation.

It is the starting point for India's pro-enforcement public-policy doctrine.

1991 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Y Narasimha Rao v Y Venkata Lakshmi

Recognition ordinarily requires jurisdiction and a ground of relief recognised by the matrimonial law governing the parties, a decision on merits, fair notice and opportunity, and absence of fraud or breach of Indian law.

It converts CPC section 13 principles into a matrimonial-recognition checklist.

1975 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Satya v Teja Singh

A foreign judgment founded on a fraudulent assumption of jurisdiction or denial of a real opportunity to contest is not entitled to recognition merely because it is formally valid in the foreign forum.

It is a foundational Indian conflict-of-laws case on fraud, domicile and matrimonial recognition.

Barcelona Traction, Light and Power Company, Limited

The Court generally linked corporate diplomatic protection to the State of incorporation and distinguished obligations owed to the international community as a whole.

It is foundational for diplomatic protection, corporate nationality and obligations erga omnes.
