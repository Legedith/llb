2012 · International Criminal Court · ICC-01/04-01/06

Prosecutor v Thomas Lubanga Dyilo

The ICC convicted Lubanga and later developed victim-participation and reparation principles, while debate continued about charging strategy and sexual violence evidence.

It is the ICC's first trial judgment and a practical study of child-soldier offences and reparations.

1999 · International Court of Justice · Citation to verify

Difference Relating to Immunity from Legal Process of a Special Rapporteur of the Commission on Human Rights

Functional immunity attaches to words and acts performed in the course of the mission, and the competent United Nations determination carries substantial weight; the host state must respect treaty obligations and available settlement procedures.

It maps functional necessity, experts on mission and the difference between immunity and impunity.

1998 · International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda · ICTR-96-4-T

Prosecutor v Jean-Paul Akayesu

The Trial Chamber entered the first international conviction for genocide and recognised rape and sexual violence as capable of constituting acts of genocide when committed with the required intent.

It is a landmark on genocide, sexual violence and contextual proof.

Prosecutor v Dusko Tadic (Jurisdiction Decision)

The Appeals Chamber articulated a broad armed-conflict threshold and upheld the tribunal's establishment and jurisdiction.

1971 · International Court of Justice · Citation to verify

Legal Consequences for States of the Continued Presence of South Africa in Namibia

South Africa's continued presence was illegal; states were obliged not to recognise or assist the unlawful situation while taking account of the interests of Namibia's people.

The opinion is central to Security Council authority, non-recognition and legal consequences of institutional decisions.

Certain Expenses of the United Nations

The Court treated peacekeeping expenditures undertaken to fulfil UN purposes as organisational expenses, while distinguishing Security Council and General Assembly competences.

It is a key authority on institutional powers, Charter interpretation and UN finance.

1954 · International Court of Justice · Citation to verify

Effect of Awards of Compensation Made by the United Nations Administrative Tribunal

The organisation's constituent framework and implied powers supported creation of an independent tribunal whose final awards bound the organisation; institutional effectiveness and justice for staff required legal effect.

It is a core application of implied powers, internal adjudication and institutional autonomy.
