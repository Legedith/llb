2017 · Supreme Court of India · (2017) 14 SCC 80

Energy Watchdog v Central Electricity Regulatory Commission

The Court held that a mere rise in cost does not frustrate a contract; the force-majeure clause and contractual allocation of risk govern before section 56 is considered.

It is the modern commercial authority on force majeure, hardship and contractual risk allocation.

Novartis AG v Union of India

The Court rejected the claim because the evidence did not establish enhanced therapeutic efficacy over the known substance and carefully distinguished patentability from general improvement.

It is the landmark Indian pharmaceutical-patent and anti-evergreening case.

2012 · Supreme Court of India · (2012) 9 SCC 552

BALCO v Kaiser Aluminium Technical Services Inc

The Constitution Bench adopted the territoriality principle and held prospectively that Part I does not apply to foreign-seated arbitrations, subject to later statutory amendments for limited interim support.

It is the foundational Indian seat doctrine.

India — Quantitative Restrictions on Imports of Agricultural, Textile and Industrial Products

The panels and Appellate Body found that the restrictions were not justified under the claimed exception and required their removal.

United States — Import Prohibition of Certain Shrimp and Shrimp Products

The measure pursued a permitted environmental objective but its discriminatory application initially failed the chapeau; later compliance focused on flexibility and good-faith negotiation.

It is the classic WTO case on environmental exceptions, process regulation and non-discrimination.
