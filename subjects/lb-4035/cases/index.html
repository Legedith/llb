2012 · International Criminal Court · ICC-01/04-01/06

Prosecutor v Thomas Lubanga Dyilo

The ICC convicted Lubanga and later developed victim-participation and reparation principles, while debate continued about charging strategy and sexual violence evidence.

It is the ICC's first trial judgment and a practical study of child-soldier offences and reparations.

2002 · International Court of Justice · ICJ Reports 2002, p 3

Arrest Warrant of 11 April 2000 (Democratic Republic of the Congo v Belgium)

The Court held that incumbent high-ranking officials enjoy personal immunity before foreign national courts while in office, without equating immunity with impunity.

It is central to universal jurisdiction, immunities and the distinction between jurisdictional bar and substantive responsibility.

1998 · International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda · ICTR-96-4-T

Prosecutor v Jean-Paul Akayesu

The Trial Chamber entered the first international conviction for genocide and recognised rape and sexual violence as capable of constituting acts of genocide when committed with the required intent.

It is a landmark on genocide, sexual violence and contextual proof.

1996 · Supreme Court of India · (1996) 1 SCC 742

National Human Rights Commission v State of Arunachal Pradesh

The Court required protection of life and liberty, restrained forcible eviction except through lawful process and directed authorities to process citizenship applications according to law.

It is the leading Indian refugee-protection authority grounded in Articles 14 and 21.

1995 · International Criminal Tribunal for the former Yugoslavia · ICTY Appeals Chamber, IT-94-1-AR72

Prosecutor v Dusko Tadic (Jurisdiction Decision)

The Appeals Chamber articulated a broad armed-conflict threshold and upheld the tribunal's establishment and jurisdiction.

It is the leading modern authority on conflict classification and institutional criminal jurisdiction.

1991 · Supreme Court of India · (1991) 3 SCC 554

Louis De Raedt v Union of India

The Court recognised Article 21 protection but held that the citizen-specific right to reside and settle does not extend to foreigners, whose stay remains governed by law.

It is an important boundary case for refugee and migration claims.

1986 · International Court of Justice · ICJ Reports 1986, p 14

Military and Paramilitary Activities in and against Nicaragua (Nicaragua v United States)

The Court found violations of the prohibition on force and non-intervention, distinguished treaty from customary obligations and required conditions such as an armed attack and request for collective self-defence.

It is the central modern authority on use of force, non-intervention, custom and attribution.
