2017 · Supreme Court of India · (2018) 2 SCC 1

Toyota Jidosha Kabushiki Kaisha v Prius Auto Industries Ltd

The Court required evidence that the mark's reputation had penetrated the Indian market at the relevant time and refused to assume worldwide fame automatically established local goodwill.

It is the modern territorial qualification on transborder-reputation claims.

2004 · Supreme Court of India · (2004) 6 SCC 145

Satyam Infoway Ltd v Sifynet Solutions Pvt Ltd

The Court held that domain names can acquire goodwill and source significance and may be protected against deceptive similarity.

It is the foundational Indian domain-name case.

2001 · Supreme Court of India · (2001) 5 SCC 73

Cadila Health Care Ltd v Cadila Pharmaceuticals Ltd

The Court required a stricter approach because confusion in medicines may endanger health and listed factors including mark nature, resemblance, consumer class, purchase mode and surrounding circumstances.

It is the principal Indian deceptive-similarity framework.

1996 · Supreme Court of India · (1996) 5 SCC 714

N R Dongre v Whirlpool Corporation

The Court upheld interim protection on evidence that the mark's reputation had reached India and that local use risked deception.

It is a leading transborder-reputation authority, later refined by territoriality cases.

1990 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Wander Ltd v Antox India P Ltd

Appellate interference is limited where the first court exercised discretion judicially on relevant principles; a different possible view is not enough unless the order is arbitrary, capricious, perverse or legally mistaken.

It separates the three injunction factors from the distinct standard governing appellate review of discretion.
