2024 · Supreme Court of India · 2024 INSC 113

Association for Democratic Reforms v Union of India

The Court invalidated the scheme and connected amendments, holding that the information restrictions were disproportionate and that unlimited corporate contributions created serious equality concerns.

It is the modern anchor on political-finance transparency, voter information and constitutional review of election funding.

2021 · Supreme Court of India · (2021) 9 SCC 449

Tata Consultancy Services Ltd v Cyrus Investments Pvt Ltd

The Court restored the NCLT's dismissal, emphasising the statutory threshold for oppression and the limits of substituting judicial business judgment for lawful corporate governance.

It is a major contemporary authority on oppression, quasi-partnership claims and remedial limits.

2019 · Supreme Court of India · [2019] 3 SCR 535

Swiss Ribbons Pvt Ltd v Union of India

The Court substantially upheld the Code, treating insolvency resolution rather than debt recovery as its central objective and accepting differentiated treatment based on relevant economic roles.

It is the leading constitutional and structural account of India's insolvency regime.

2012 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Aneeta Hada v Godfather Travels and Tours Pvt Ltd

Where liability is founded on the statutory vicarious-liability provision, arraigning and establishing the offence of the company is ordinarily a necessary condition before responsible officers can be held liable, subject to legally recognised exceptions such as impossibility.

It is the anchor for entity-first analysis in cheque-dishonour company cases.

Sahara India Real Estate Corporation Ltd v Securities and Exchange Board of India

Substance, number and manner of offerees, disclosure and statutory thresholds determine whether an issue is public; labels and corporate structuring cannot evade investor-protection duties and SEBI jurisdiction.

It is a major case study in regulatory perimeter, disclosure and restitution.

2012 · Supreme Court of India · (2012) 6 SCC 613

Vodafone International Holdings BV v Union of India

The majority held that the transaction fell outside the then statutory charge and emphasised legal structure absent sham; Parliament later enacted retrospective amendments.

It is central to source rules, indirect transfers, corporate structure and the distinction between judicial interpretation and legislative response.

1986 · Supreme Court of India · (1986) 1 SCC 264

Life Insurance Corporation of India v Escorts Ltd

The Court recognised that veil lifting is exceptional and depends on statute, fraud, evasion, agency or the demands of the particular legal rule; it is not a general licence to ignore incorporation.

It is a leading Indian statement of veil-piercing limits.

1981 · Supreme Court of India · (1981) 3 SCC 333

Needle Industries (India) Ltd v Needle Industries Newey (India) Holding Ltd

Oppression requires burdensome, harsh and wrongful conduct viewed in context; the court may grant equitable relief even where technical illegality and oppression do not perfectly overlap.

It remains a foundational Indian authority on oppression, good faith and remedial flexibility.

Salomon v A Salomon & Co Ltd

The company was a separate legal person with its own rights and liabilities, notwithstanding concentrated ownership.
