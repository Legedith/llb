Media and Law

2020 · Supreme Court of India · (2020) 3 SCC 637

Anuradha Bhasin v Union of India

The Court held that freedom of speech and trade through the internet receive constitutional protection, indefinite suspension is impermissible, orders must be published and restrictions require periodic proportionality review.

It is the principal Indian authority on internet shutdowns, publication of executive orders and digital proportionality.

2015 · Supreme Court of India · [2015] 5 SCR 963

Shreya Singhal v Union of India

The Court struck down section 66A for vagueness and overbreadth, upheld section 69A with safeguards and read actual knowledge for intermediary takedown as requiring court or authorised governmental process.

It is the foundational case for online speech, vagueness, blocking and intermediary safe harbour.

2009 · Supreme Court of India · (2009) 8 SCC 106

R K Anand v Registrar, Delhi High Court

The Court upheld contempt findings, examined professional misconduct and stressed the duty of advocates to preserve trial integrity.

It is a vivid case study in witness tampering, media evidence, contempt and professional ethics.

1995 · Supreme Court of India · (1995) 2 SCC 161

Secretary, Ministry of Information and Broadcasting v Cricket Association of Bengal

The Court recognised broadcasting freedom and the public's right to receive information, while treating airwaves as public property requiring independent and public-interest regulation.

It is the foundational Indian broadcast-freedom case.

1994 · Supreme Court of India · (1994) 6 SCC 632

R Rajagopal v State of Tamil Nadu

The Court recognised a privacy interest against unauthorised publication of private life but protected publication based on public records and rejected prior restraint by officials.

It is the foundational Indian privacy-and-media case.

1978 · Supreme Court of India · (1978) 4 SCC 118

R G Anand v Deluxe Films

Copyright does not protect a general idea, theme or subject; infringement depends on substantial similarity in protected expression assessed through the overall impression and material similarities.

It is the foundational Indian copyright-infringement test.

1972 · Supreme Court of India · (1972) 2 SCC 788

Bennett Coleman & Co v Union of India

The Court held that the policy's direct effect on pages, circulation and newspaper growth infringed freedom of the press.

It is a leading authority on indirect burdens, corporate press claims and effects-based review.

1950 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1950 SC 124

Romesh Thappar v State of Madras

The Court invalidated the restriction because the statutory ground did not fit the then constitutional limitation and treated circulation as part of freedom of speech.

It is an early foundational press-freedom and strict-grounds case.
