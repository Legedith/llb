2019 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

M Siddiq (D) through LRs v Mahant Suresh Das

The Court treated the deity as a juristic person, assessed documentary and archaeological evidence, distinguished faith from proof of title, and fashioned relief through the law of property and constitutional authority.

It is a rich case study in legal personality, evidence, possession and remedial design.

Satya Pal Anand v State of Madhya Pradesh

The Court held that the registration authority's powers are limited; substantive invalidity or cancellation generally requires proceedings before the competent civil court.

It is a practical authority on registration officers, title disputes and remedies.

2014 · Supreme Court of India · (2014) 9 SCC 445

Renikuntla Rajamma v K Sarwanamma

A registered and accepted gift may be valid even when the donor reserves a life interest or possession; transfer of ownership is the controlling question.

It clarifies acceptance, registration and reservation of enjoyment in gift deeds.

2011 · Supreme Court of India · (2011) 5 SCC 532

Booz Allen and Hamilton Inc v SBI Home Finance Ltd

The Court distinguished rights in personam from rights in rem and held that mortgage enforcement through sale was not arbitrable in the circumstances.

It is the classic Indian arbitrability framework, later refined by Vidya Drolia.

2011 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

K T Plantation Pvt Ltd v State of Karnataka

Deprivation of property requires authority of valid law; public purpose, non-arbitrariness and the character of compensation may be relevant to constitutional review, although Article 300A is not identical to the former fundamental right.

It connects ownership theory to the modern constitutional status of property.

Suraj Lamp & Industries Pvt Ltd v State of Haryana

Such arrangements do not convey title or amount to a completed transfer, though genuine powers of attorney, development arrangements and contracts may retain their lawful effects.

2008 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

K B Saha and Sons Pvt Ltd v Development Consultant Ltd

An unregistered instrument required to be registered cannot prove the transaction affecting immovable property, but may be admitted for a genuinely collateral matter independent of the transaction that required registration.

It supplies a practical test for the often-misused collateral-purpose exception.

2004 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Karnataka Board of Wakf v Government of India

Possession must be actual, open, continuous, exclusive and hostile to the true owner's title for the statutory period; the claimant must identify when and how hostility began and cannot rely on vague long possession alone.

It supplies the pleading and proof checklist for adverse possession.

1977 · Supreme Court of India · (1977) 3 SCC 247

Narandas Karsondas v S A Kamtam

A contract for sale does not itself create an interest in immovable property; redemption survives until extinguished by act of parties or a completed sale under law.

It connects mortgage redemption, sale agreements and the distinction between contractual and proprietary rights.

Jayaram Mudaliar v Ayyaswami

The transfer is not void but remains subject to the rights determined in the pending proceeding; the doctrine protects the court's ability to decide effectively.

It is the standard authority on the operation and rationale of lis pendens.

Associated Hotels of India Ltd v R N Kapoor

The substance of the arrangement, intention and transfer of a right to enjoy property control the classification; exclusive possession is important but not mechanically conclusive.
