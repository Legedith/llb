2022 · Supreme Court of India · (2022) 10 SCC 51

Satender Kumar Antil v Central Bureau of Investigation

The Court issued category-based directions, emphasised compliance with arrest safeguards, timely bail disposal and legislative consideration of a comprehensive bail law.

It is a major current authority on bail administration and undertrial liberty.

2013 · Supreme Court of India · [2013] 14 SCR 713

Lalita Kumari v Government of Uttar Pradesh

The Court held that registration is mandatory on disclosure of a cognizable offence, with a limited preliminary inquiry permitted in specified categories to determine whether such disclosure exists.

It is the foundational FIR-registration authority and must be read with current BNSS text.

2006 · Supreme Court of India · (2006) 8 SCC 1

Prakash Singh v Union of India

The Court directed States and the Union to establish security commissions, merit-based selection, minimum tenure, separation of investigation and police complaints authorities.

It is the central police-reform authority, notable also for persistent implementation gaps.

1997 · Supreme Court of India · [1996] Supp 10 SCR 284

D K Basu v State of West Bengal

The Court prescribed arrest, documentation, notification, medical examination and production safeguards, treating non-compliance as actionable and potentially contemptuous.

It is the operational constitutional authority on arrest safeguards and custodial accountability.

1980 · Supreme Court of India · (1980) 2 SCC 684

Bachan Singh v State of Punjab

The Court upheld the penalty but confined it to the rarest of rare cases after individualised consideration of crime and criminal, with life imprisonment as the normal rule.

It is the foundational capital-sentencing authority.

1979 · Supreme Court of India · (1980) 1 SCC 81

Hussainara Khatoon v Home Secretary, State of Bihar

The Court treated speedy trial and meaningful legal aid as components of fair procedure and ordered release and systemic action.

1978 · Supreme Court of India · (1978) 4 SCC 494

Sunil Batra v Delhi Administration

The Court held that imprisonment does not extinguish constitutional rights, restricted solitary confinement and opened judicial oversight of prison treatment.

It is a foundational prisoners' rights and epistolary-jurisdiction case.

1961 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1962 SC 605

K M Nanavati v State of Maharashtra

The Court held that the sequence and interval showed deliberation rather than a legally sufficient continuing provocation, and restored the murder conviction.

It is the iconic provocation case and also illustrates jury-era criminal procedure.
