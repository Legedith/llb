2018 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Commissioner of Customs (Import), Mumbai v Dilip Kumar and Company

The claimant must establish that the exemption applies; unresolved ambiguity in an exemption notification is ordinarily construed in favour of the revenue, while ambiguity in the charging provision follows different principles.

It is the principal modern authority distinguishing charging provisions from exemption claims.

2012 · Supreme Court of India · (2012) 6 SCC 613

Vodafone International Holdings BV v Union of India

The majority held that the transaction fell outside the then statutory charge and emphasised legal structure absent sham; Parliament later enacted retrospective amendments.

It is central to source rules, indirect transfers, corporate structure and the distinction between judicial interpretation and legislative response.

2003 · Supreme Court of India · (2004) 10 SCC 1

Union of India v Azadi Bachao Andolan

The Court upheld the circular and rejected the proposition that every tax-avoidance arrangement is illegitimate; legal form and treaty entitlement matter unless the law supplies a contrary rule.

It is the principal corrective to overbroad readings of McDowell.

1985 · Supreme Court of India · (1985) 3 SCC 230

McDowell and Co Ltd v Commercial Tax Officer

The judgments condemned colourable devices, but later authority clarified that legitimate tax planning remains permissible and that McDowell should not be read as a universal anti-avoidance rule.

It is important chiefly when read together with Azadi Bachao Andolan and Vodafone.

1981 · Supreme Court of India · (1981) 4 SCC 173

K P Varghese v Income Tax Officer

The Court used text, statutory purpose and legislative history to require proof of understatement rather than taxing a fictional receipt.

It is both a tax case and a leading authority on purposive interpretation and use of external aids.

currentDirect source link
1961 · Supreme Court of India · (1961) 2 SCR 634

Commissioner of Income Tax v Sitaldas Tirathdas

Income is excluded only where an overriding obligation diverts it at source; a personal obligation to apply income after it accrues does not prevent taxation.

It is the foundational income-diversion test.

1958 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1958 SC 560

State of Madras v Gannon Dunkerley & Co

The Court held that the traditional sale concept required an agreement to sell goods and transfer of property as goods; Parliament later altered the constitutional framework for works contracts.

It is a classic example of sale classification, legislative response and constitutional amendment.
