2019 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Pioneer Urban Land and Infrastructure Ltd v Govindan Raghavan

A dominant developer cannot rely on manifestly one-sided terms to defeat consumer relief; delay and unfair allocation of remedies may justify refund, interest and compensation under consumer law.

It links adhesion contracts, housing regulation and consumer remedies.

2019 · Supreme Court of India · [2019] 7 SCR 522

Ssangyong Engineering & Construction Co Ltd v National Highways Authority of India

The Court narrowed public-policy review, explained patent illegality for domestic awards and set aside the relevant part because the tribunal effectively created a new bargain contrary to the contract and natural justice.

It is the leading post-2015 award-review authority.

2017 · Supreme Court of India · (2017) 14 SCC 80

Energy Watchdog v Central Electricity Regulatory Commission

The Court held that a mere rise in cost does not frustrate a contract; the force-majeure clause and contractual allocation of risk govern before section 56 is considered.

It is the modern commercial authority on force majeure, hardship and contractual risk allocation.

Nabha Power Ltd v Punjab State Power Corporation Ltd

A term may be implied only where strict necessity and the recognised business-efficacy conditions are met; courts should not rewrite a negotiated commercial instrument.

It is a leading modern guide to contractual interpretation and implied terms.

2015 · Supreme Court of India · [2015] 1 SCR 627

Kailash Nath Associates v Delhi Development Authority

The Court held that forfeiture requires breach and reasonable compensation; where loss is capable of proof it should be proved, and a public authority cannot retain money arbitrarily.

It synthesises Fateh Chand and section 74 for modern contracts and public auctions.

Export Credit Guarantee Corporation of India Ltd v Garg Sons International

Courts interpret the contract but do not make a new policy for the parties; clear limitations and conditions must be applied, subject to statute and recognised interpretive principles.

It is useful for the boundary between interpretation and rewriting an insurance bargain.

Trimex International FZE Ltd v Vedanta Aluminium Ltd

The Court treated the essential terms and unconditional electronic acceptance as capable of creating a binding contract; contemplation of a formal document did not necessarily postpone formation.

2003 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Modi Entertainment Network v WSG Cricket Pte Ltd

An anti-suit injunction is an equitable order against a party, not the foreign court. It requires personal jurisdiction, caution, comity, prevention of injustice and a strong case that the chosen foreign proceeding is oppressive, vexatious or contrary to a binding forum arrangement.

It is the principal Indian framework for anti-suit injunctions and forum clauses.

1963 · Supreme Court of India · AIR 1963 SC 1405

Fateh Chand v Balkishan Das

Section 74 awards reasonable compensation up to the named amount; it does not permit automatic recovery or forfeiture disconnected from breach and legally cognisable loss.

It is the leading foundation for penalty clauses and reasonable compensation.

Impossibility includes impracticability that strikes at the foundation of the bargain, but the requisition on the facts did not necessarily frustrate the contract.

1903 · Judicial Committee of the Privy Council · [1903] UKPC 12

Mohori Bibee v Dharmodas Ghose

An agreement with a minor is void from the beginning because a minor is not competent to contract; restitution cannot be used to enforce the void bargain indirectly.

It is the foundational capacity case and must be read with statutory rules on necessaries and restitution.
