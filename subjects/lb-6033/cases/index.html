2026 · Supreme Court of India · 2026 INSC 564

Association for Democratic Reforms v Election Commission of India

The Court upheld the revision, treated Article 324 and the Representation of the People Act as complementary, recognised a limited electoral-eligibility inquiry into citizenship and required referral of final citizenship determination to the competent authority.

It is a major current authority on electoral-roll revision, proportionality, presumptions and the limits of Election Commission power.

Association for Democratic Reforms v Union of India

The Court invalidated the scheme and connected amendments, holding that the information restrictions were disproportionate and that unlimited corporate contributions created serious equality concerns.

2020 · Supreme Court of India · (2020) 7 SCC 1

Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal

The Court reaffirmed the certificate requirement for secondary electronic evidence, clarified that it may be produced at an appropriate stage and recognised court assistance where the record is controlled by another person.

It is the present leading authority on electronic-record certification under the predecessor statute, requiring crosswalk to the BSA.

Keisham Meghachandra Singh v Hon'ble Speaker, Manipur Legislative Assembly

2014 · Supreme Court of India · (2014) 10 SCC 473

Anvar P V v P K Basheer

The Court required compliance with the electronic-record certificate provision for the relevant category of secondary electronic evidence and displaced contrary reasoning in State v Navjot Sandhu.

It reset Indian electronic-evidence doctrine and was later clarified by Arjun Panditrao.

Union of India v Association for Democratic Reforms

The Court held that informed voting is protected by Article 19(1)(a) and directed disclosure of specified candidate information.

1992 · Supreme Court of India · [1992] 1 SCR 686

Kihoto Hollohan v Zachillhu

The Court largely upheld the Tenth Schedule while holding that final decisions of the Speaker are subject to judicial review on recognised grounds.

It is the starting point for anti-defection adjudication and the constitutional status of the Speaker.

1978 · Supreme Court of India · (1978) 1 SCC 405

Mohinder Singh Gill v Chief Election Commissioner

The Court recognised broad residual election-management power within law and stated that an order must stand or fall on the reasons contained in it, not later affidavits.

It is central to election administration and the general administrative-law rule against post-hoc reasons.

1975 · Supreme Court of India · 1975 Supp SCC 1

Indira Nehru Gandhi v Raj Narain

The Court invalidated the immunity provision through basic-structure reasoning, emphasising free and fair elections, equality and judicial review.

It is the foundational connection between election law and basic structure.
