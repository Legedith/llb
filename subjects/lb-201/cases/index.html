2026 · Supreme Court of India · 2026 INSC 564

Association for Democratic Reforms v Election Commission of India

The Court upheld the revision, treated Article 324 and the Representation of the People Act as complementary, recognised a limited electoral-eligibility inquiry into citizenship and required referral of final citizenship determination to the competent authority.

It is a major current authority on electoral-roll revision, proportionality, presumptions and the limits of Election Commission power.

Neeraj Dutta v State (Government of NCT of Delhi)

The Court held that demand and acceptance remain essential but may be proved through direct or circumstantial evidence; a hostile or unavailable complainant does not automatically end the prosecution.

It is the present leading authority on proof in corruption cases.

Arjun Panditrao Khotkar v Kailash Kushanrao Gorantyal

The Court reaffirmed the certificate requirement for secondary electronic evidence, clarified that it may be produced at an appropriate stage and recognised court assistance where the record is controlled by another person.

It is the present leading authority on electronic-record certification under the predecessor statute, requiring crosswalk to the BSA.

The majority upheld acquittal on the then record and legal framework. The decision became a major negative precedent that triggered statutory reforms on consent, custody and evidential burdens.

Pulukuri Kottaya v Emperor

Only so much of the information as distinctly relates to the fact discovered is admissible; the exception does not admit a full confession merely because discovery follows.

It is the controlling foundation for discovery statements.
