2019 · Supreme Court of India · (2019) 15 SCC 401

Hanuman Laxman Aroskar v Union of India

The Court required reasoned, transparent and informed appraisal, treated environmental rule of law as central and suspended the clearance for reconsideration.

It is a modern authority on EIA procedure, disclosure and reasons.

2017 · Uttarakhand High Court · Citation to verify

Mohd Salim v State of Uttarakhand

The High Court used juristic personality as a protective device, but the order's operation and practical implications were questioned and stayed in later Supreme Court proceedings. It should be studied as an ambitious but unsettled personhood experiment.

It is valuable precisely because it shows both the attraction and institutional limits of nature personhood.

2014 · Supreme Court of India · Citation to verify

Animal Welfare Board of India v A Nagaraja

Animal-welfare statutes must be applied to prevent unnecessary pain and protect animal well-being; constitutional duties and an ecocentric approach may inform interpretation, although the precise constitutional status of animal rights remains doctrinally contested and later legislation must be checked.

It is a major bridge between legal personhood debates, statutory welfare and constitutional duties.

1999 · Supreme Court of India · (1999) 2 SCC 718

A P Pollution Control Board v M V Nayudu

The Court emphasised precaution, the limits of conventional adjudication and the need for expert environmental decision-making.

It is an anchor for precaution under uncertainty, expert bodies and environmental evidence.

1998 · WTO Appellate Body · WT/DS58

United States — Import Prohibition of Certain Shrimp and Shrimp Products

The measure pursued a permitted environmental objective but its discriminatory application initially failed the chapeau; later compliance focused on flexibility and good-faith negotiation.

It is the classic WTO case on environmental exceptions, process regulation and non-discrimination.

1997 · Supreme Court of India · (1997) 1 SCC 388

M C Mehta v Kamal Nath

The Court applied the public-trust doctrine, held that the State holds certain natural resources for public use and ordered restoration and compensation.

It is the principal Indian public-trust case.

Indian Council for Enviro-Legal Action v Union of India

The Court imposed responsibility for remediation on the offending industries and reinforced polluter-pays and absolute-liability reasoning.

1996 · Supreme Court of India · [1996] Supp 5 SCR 241

Vellore Citizens Welfare Forum v Union of India

The Court treated sustainable development, precaution and polluter pays as part of Indian environmental law and ordered remedial and compensation measures.

It is the canonical Indian authority on core environmental principles.

1987 · Supreme Court of India · (1987) 1 SCC 395

M C Mehta v Union of India (Oleum Gas Leak)

The Court formulated absolute and non-delegable enterprise liability, without the traditional Rylands exceptions, and linked compensation to the enterprise's capacity and the magnitude of harm.

It is the foundational Indian authority on absolute liability, constitutional tort technique and hazardous industry.
